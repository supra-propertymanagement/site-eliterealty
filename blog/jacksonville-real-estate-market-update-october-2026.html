Buying or Selling in Jacksonville This Winter: October 2026 Market Update

Steady prices, more homes to choose from, and mortgage rates above 7%. Here’s how to make the most of the season.

A lot of people assume real estate goes into hibernation between Thanksgiving and New Year’s. In Jacksonville, it doesn’t. Homes keep selling through the winter, and the people shopping now tend to be serious: relocating families, military moves, and buyers who want to be settled before spring.

The latest numbers from October show a Northeast Florida market that’s holding its value while giving buyers more breathing room than they’ve had in years. Here’s what the data says, and what it means for your plans this winter.

The numbers: October 2026

According to the Northeast Florida Association of REALTORS® (NEFAR), which tracks Duval, St. Johns, Clay, Nassau, Putnam, and Baker counties, here’s how single-family homes did in October:

  • Median sales price: about $399,000, essentially unchanged from September
  • Median days on market: 36, the same as September
  • Closed sales: 1,477, down from 1,581 in September
  • Pending sales: 1,501
  • New listings: 2,208
  • Active inventory: 6,531 homes, a 4.4-month supply (up from 4.0)

Compared with a year ago, the regional median price is up roughly 4%, while the number of closed sales is down about 15%. In other words, prices are holding firm even as fewer homes change hands. NEFAR President Kim Knapp summed it up this way: buyers have options, and sellers are navigating a more competitive environment.

Each county in Northeast Florida behaves a little differently. Here’s how they compared in October:

St. Johns is the tightest market in the region, with under four months of inventory even at the highest prices. Duval, the region’s biggest market by far, still moves quickly: homes there sold in a median of about a month. Nassau and Putnam have the most inventory, which gives buyers in those counties the most room to negotiate. Baker’s big price jump comes from only 14 sales, so treat it with caution. Small counties can swing a lot from month to month.

Mortgage rates: the other half of the story

Mortgage rates are the biggest reason sales have slowed. Freddie Mac’s weekly survey put the average 30-year fixed rate at 7.11% as of November 12, down slightly from the week before, but well above the 6.24% average a year earlier. Statewide, Florida REALTORS® reported fewer October sales as higher rates weighed on buyers, while the median single-family price held at $420,000, up 2.2% from a year ago.

Most economists aren’t expecting a quick drop in rates, with inflation still running above the Federal Reserve’s target. Waiting for rates to fall is a gamble. A better plan is to buy the right home at the right price now and refinance later if rates come down.

If you’re buying this winter

  • You have leverage. With a 4.4-month supply and fewer buyers out shopping during the holidays, you’re less likely to face bidding wars. It’s reasonable to ask for repairs, help with closing costs, or a seller-paid rate buydown.
  • Ask about rate buydowns. A seller credit that buys down your interest rate can lower your monthly payment more than an equal cut to the price. Your lender can run both scenarios side by side.
  • Get pre-approved first. A pre-approval shows sellers you’re serious, and it tells you exactly what monthly payment you’re comfortable with at today’s rates.
  • Moving to Florida? Watch December 31. Under Amendment 3, being a permanent Florida resident by December 31, 2026 matters for the larger homestead exemption. Read our Amendment 3 breakdown and talk to the property appraiser’s office about your situation.

If you’re selling this winter

  • Less competition from other sellers. Many homeowners wait until spring to list. Listing now means your home stands out to the motivated buyers who are shopping.
  • Price it right from day one. Buyers have choices, so overpriced homes sit. Homes priced for their condition and neighborhood are still selling in about five weeks.
  • Consider offering a rate buydown. With rates above 7%, offering to help with the buyer’s interest rate can attract more buyers than a price cut, and it can cost you less.
  • Make it shine. Clean, bright, and move-in ready still wins. Our guide on how to prepare your house for sale walks through the essentials.

If you’re investing

More inventory and fewer competing buyers create opportunities, especially in counties with longer days on market. Under Amendment 3, the annual cap on assessment increases for non-homestead property drops from 10% to 5% for non-school taxes starting in 2027, which makes long-term rental holds easier to plan around. Run your numbers at today’s rates, and plan for a refinance as an upside, not a requirement.

The bottom line

Northeast Florida heads into winter with stable prices, more homes to choose from, and serious buyers still in the market. Rates are higher than anyone would like, but that’s exactly why the details matter: how a home is priced, how an offer is structured, and when you make your move.
